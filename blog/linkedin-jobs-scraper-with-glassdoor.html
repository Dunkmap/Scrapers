Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to scrape 10,000 LinkedIn jobs?

On the Apify free plan it costs $4.00 for 10,000 jobs ($0.40 per 1,000), with full details included. Higher Apify plans pay less, down to $0.30 per 1,000 jobs.

Do I need a LinkedIn account or cookies?

No. The scraper reads public job listings over plain HTTP, with no login, no cookies and no browser.

Does it remove duplicates between LinkedIn and Glassdoor?

Yes. When the same job appears on both sites, it is kept once, so your dataset has one row per job.

Can it collect only new jobs every day?

Yes. Turn on "only new jobs" and schedule a daily run. Jobs collected in earlier runs are skipped and not charged.

Which formats can I export?

JSON, CSV and Excel from the Apify console, or the same data through the Apify API, n8n, Make or Zapier.

Can AI agents use it?

Yes. It works in ChatGPT, Claude and n8n, and as an MCP server, so AI agents can run job searches and get structured results back.

Step 1 — Run a small test

  1. Open the Actor on Apify.
  2. Under Job titles or keywords, type a role, for example Data Analyst.
  3. Under Locations, type a place, for example Bangalore. Always add a location — Glassdoor needs one, and without it only LinkedIn runs.
  4. Leave Max jobs per site at the default 100 and click Start.

Jobs begin appearing in the Output tab within a minute. Starting small lets you check the results before you spend more.

How the filters work

Filters run from cheapest to most expensive, and a job that is removed early is never charged.

  • The site appliesPosted within, early applicants (LinkedIn), and company rating, size and search radius (Glassdoor). Glassdoor also applies work type, job type and experience level; LinkedIn ignores those three and the run warns you.
  • The job card appliesOnly new jobs, one job per company, only new companies, match titles to my keywords, exclude title words and exclude internships.
  • The company page appliesMinimum company size and exclude industries. Exact on LinkedIn with full job details on; on Glassdoor, industries are matched on the company name, which catches most but not all.

Full job details

Get full job details opens each job and company page. You get the full description, applicant count, seniority, industry, skills, pay found in the description, company size, employee count and website. With Include job poster on, you also get the recruiter’s name and profile link when LinkedIn shows them, and emails written in the posting. These appear on a minority of jobs, never as a promise.

Step 3 — Stay out of duplicates

  • Only new jobs remembers the jobs you already received for 60 days.
  • Only new companies remembers companies for 30 days by default. Change Company history to 7, 90 days or never. “Never” gives every company once, ever, but a daily schedule then runs out of new companies.
  • Remove duplicates across sites is on by default.

Step 4 — Run it every day and send it to a sheet

  1. Click Save as a new task.
  2. Add a Schedule, for example every morning.
  3. In the task’s Integrations tab, add Google Sheets Import & Export, choose Append mode, and paste your sheet’s ID.

New jobs now land in the same sheet every day. You can also use the Actor from n8n or Make, or from Claude and ChatGPT through the Apify MCP server.

If you get too few results

  • Add a location, or remove some filters. The run log names the filter that removed the most jobs.
  • With many filters on, most jobs are removed. Loosen Posted within first, then Only new companies, then the company size.
  • Add other names for the same role as extra keywords, for example “Full Stack Developer” and “Full Stack Engineer”, because the title match is strict.
  • LinkedIn returns roughly 850 to 1,000 jobs per keyword and location. Use more keywords or locations to go wider.
  • For very large keyword lists, split them into runs of about 100 keywords, because Apify stops runs after one hour by default.

Is it legal?

The Actor reads publicly visible job postings and never collects candidate profiles or resumes. As with any data collection, use the results lawfully and follow the sites’ terms and your local laws.
